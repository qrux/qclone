qclone v4, as built

2026-10-13 · repo ~/proj/qrux/qclone (github.com/qrux/qclone) · not yet released (the Homebrew tap still serves 0.1.0) · written by the Claude Code session that built it

This covers what changed after qclone-v3-built.html (code at 9cab425, documented at 4db68a6). Everything in v1 to v3 still holds unless this document says otherwise; in particular v3's "stuck room" (flying + min(stuck, 16)) is replaced. As before, there was no spec: the design came out of a conversation with Q on 2026-10-13, quoted below.

In one line: each 32 MiB of a file is a region, judged fresh or slow by its reads. Reads in flight never exceed a queue length Q measured at the start. Within it, a slow lane of R reads (measured, at most Q − 4) is kept for slow regions; W flying reads take fresh regions; when there's no fresh work, tries probe unknown regions breadth first; and until something fresh turns up, slow reads fill every slot left. Once fresh work exists, slow reads back off to R. Demo on the simulated drive: 48 s in hash order, no errors, byte-identical.

1. What was asked

The v3 design capped stuck reads at 16 beside an uncapped flying number. Q pointed out it couldn't hold: any read can turn out stuck, so stuck reads grow past any cap, and on a SATA drive every slot of its 32-command queue can end up holding one. The conversation that followed, in Q's words:

  • "For stuck reads, they go into a room where they can only occupy up to 32 slots. Then, one by one, down to the size of the 'stuck room' (which should be determined by *ACTUALLY* parallel the drive is ...), where the stuck reads manually ... sit in a queue, so that qclone is 'piling them into a queue' rather than NCQ or macOS. This leave room to add new attempts in those freed slots."
  • "But we're assuming drive/enclosure properties. Isn't there some way to get a handle on what the *ACTUAL* values are?"
  • "Briefly above R, but always below (or at) NCQ. Once we reach NCQ = S (stuck readers), then YOU/qclone MANUALLY backs them off ... Those files ... are basically 'quarantined' ... if the ENTIRE DRIVE has been scanned, and literally there are no more fast files/regions, then we give up, and just ride R slots until it finishes."
  • "As long as S > R, then R slots are reserved for slow files/regions/whatever."
  • "Pathologically, if NCQ = R, then we manually set R' = (R - 4), and use R' as the new R, saving 4 slots for fast reads."
  • On starting the backoff at R: "I disagree that we get nothing. It means that macOS/kernel delivers the next command—which is faster than you doing it—UNTIL we find a faster one. Once we find faster ones, then we start the quarantine process. Until then, having more slow readers also doesn't hurt."
  • On looking inside big files for fresh stretches: "Do a BFS style descent over regions, then."
  • Q's degraded drive is a SATA SSD in a Thunderbolt 4 enclosure.

3. Choices made, and why

3.1 What a free reader does (pick)

  1. The slow lane: while fewer than R slow reads are out, a slow region's next chunk. These R slots stay with slow regions whenever they have work, however many failed tries are stuck elsewhere (Q's "as long as S > R").
  2. Flying reads: while fewer than W are out, a fresh region's next chunk. While fresh work waits, nothing else goes out ahead of it. That is the quarantine: slow reads beyond R aren't replaced as they finish, so they back off to R and their slots go to fresh reads. Nothing is ever canceled.
  3. Tries, only when there's no fresh work: the untried region of lowest rank across open files (every open file's start, then a new file's, then the next level down: middles, quarters, ...). One try per region at a time. New files open while fewer than 2Q files are open.
  4. More slow reads in every slot left, only while nothing fresh is known, nothing is flying, and no try is pending. This is Q's "until we find a faster one, more slow readers doesn't hurt": the next command is already queued at the drive.

3.2 Pending tries (found by a test)

A first version filled the queue with slow reads whenever no region was known fresh. In TestFreshFilesGoByTheSlowLane that fired in the instant the fresh files' first reads were out but not yet back: the slow file jumped to 15 reads, then took 200 ms to drain. Now a try counts as pending until it comes back or goes stuck, and rule 4 waits for no pending tries. The test passed five runs in a row afterwards, and asserts the slow lane holds at 4 (plus the try that found the file slow) while fresh work remains.

3.3 The ceilings

  • Q caps everything in flight. The gate admits Q workers; the pool has 256.
  • R ≤ Q − 4, Q's rule for the pathological case, applied always: at least 4 slots are left for fresh reads. A queue of 4 or less has no slow lane; slow regions are then read only when nothing fresh is left (rule 4).
  • W ≤ Q. No other ceiling; the tuner stops where more stops paying.
  • Open files < 2Q: tries in a folder of old files would otherwise open file after file, each left half-done.

3.4 Why measure Q this way

The number that matters is how many commands the whole path really works on at once: macOS, the enclosure's bridge, the link and the drive. A reported queue length can't show a bridge that takes fewer or passes commands on one at a time; a measurement covers every layer. Small reads show it best: SATA SSD spec sheets typically list around 10k random 4 KiB reads a second with 1 in flight and 90–100k with 32, so the rate climbs to the real limit and stops. 256 KiB chunks would saturate the link long before the queue. Calibration runs before any copying, so nothing else is in flight; it reads at most 64 MiB of the chosen file, scattered, so the drive's own cache can't flatter it.

3.5 Regions, not a look-ahead

The look-ahead inside quarantined big files that was discussed became regions: every 32 MiB is judged on its own, and tries descend breadth first. A big file with an old stretch and fresh ones gets the fresh ones read in the flying slots while the old one grinds in the slow lane (TestRegionsAreJudgedApart).

4. Where the build departs from the request

  • The end rides the full queue, not R. Q first said "ride R slots until it finishes", then that more slow readers don't hurt until something fresh turns up. With nothing fresh left, rule 4 fills the queue.
  • The reported queue length isn't read. Only the measurement is used. The IOKit property name for a drive behind a Thunderbolt enclosure is unknown; the ioreg check handed to Q (filtered for queue-related properties) hasn't been run yet.
  • Regions stand in for the look-ahead (3.5), and tries are breadth first across files as well as within them.

5. What was verified, and how

  • make test: go vet, then 60 tests with the race detector, all passing (the diagnostic is skipped by design). New:
    • TestFreshFilesGoByTheSlowLane: a slow file (200 ms reads, served in parallel) and 16 fresh files (10 ms reads); W 4, R 4, Q 16. While fresh work remained, the slow file had at most 5 reads at once (its lane of 4 plus the try); all 64 fresh chunks were done before it was half done; afterwards it filled the queue (16 at once).
    • TestSlowBacksOffWhenFreshTurnsUp: 20 old files, then 16 fresh ones the walk reaches later; Q 8, R 4. Before anything fresh, reads in flight reached 8 and never more; from 250 ms after the first fresh read until the fresh work ran out (about 400 ms), slow reads stayed at 4 or fewer.
    • TestRegionsAreJudgedApart: one file of 8 regions, 2 of them old; all 24 fresh chunks were done before the old regions were half read.
    • TestNoLanesReadsLikeRclone: one read per file, at most 4 in all.
    • TestBreadthFirst: the descent order, and every index exactly once for 0 to 199.
    • TestQueueMeasured: a stand-in drive that serves 8 reads at a time measures as 8. TestQueueNotMeasuredWithoutFreshData: all-slow data leaves Q at 32.
  • The built binary on the demo tree (972 MB, 362 files, --simulate degraded --plain): 48 s, no errors, diff -r finds no difference.
  • make tuner-diag:

6. Concerns and open issues

6.1 The simulator still can't show lanes helping

Its single shared queue makes every fresh read wait behind slow ones, so almost everything counts as stuck, and fresh regions rarely stay fresh. The engine tests show the lanes with stand-in timings; the real drive, or a simulator variant with separate slow and fresh resources, is still needed to show them on a drive model.

6.2 R is measured only in mixed stretches

R is tuned only while fresh and slow work are both waiting, and its measurements need about 3 s and 20 slow reads each. Mixed stretches shorter than that leave R at 4. On the simulator it never moved.

6.3 The queue measurement varies

The two diagnostic runs measured 16 and 24 on the same simulated drive: past its real limit (about 14 there), rates at higher counts sit near the 95% line, and noise decides. A margin either way changes little, since slots past the real limit just wait. It's measured once, at the start, on the first fresh file found within 5 s.

6.4 The region size matches the simulator's

qclone's regions and the simulator's old/fresh regions are both 32 MiB, which flatters region detection in simulation. On a real drive, ages don't follow 32 MiB boundaries; a region that straddles one turns slow at its first old chunk and fresh again when reads come back fast.

6.5 Not yet done

  • No run on the real drive (SATA SSD, Thunderbolt 4 enclosure). It will show the measured queue, whether R climbs, and whether fresh regions stay fresh beside slow reads.
  • The ioreg check of what macOS reports for the enclosure.
  • No 0.2.0 release.
